## The research question

In this scenario, the aim would be to understand how a person's expectations and the events they experience might be represented in a model of mood. Which relationships should we choose to formalise? And what would this representation leave out?

The goal would be to compare several explicit hypotheses, then identify the observations that would allow us to distinguish between them. The project would not be presented as a diagnostic tool or as a complete explanation of a person's experience.

> **The question in one sentence.** What do we learn about an experience when we try to represent it, and how can we recognise the limits of that representation?

## An approach in three steps

<Steps>

1. **Formulate the hypotheses.** Define what the model would represent: expectations, events and their possible relationships. Distinguish theoretical choices from necessary simplifications.

2. **Compare scenarios.** Explore the consequences of each hypothesis through simulations. Describe the parameters and comparison conditions so that the reasoning can be examined.

3. **Compare with observations.** Determine which observations would be relevant, and under what conditions they could be collected and interpreted. No data collection takes place in this example.

</Steps>
